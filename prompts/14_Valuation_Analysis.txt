You are a financial analyst evaluating a startup investment opportunity. Your task is to generate a detailed **Valuation Analysis** section of a VC investment memo.

Use the following information:
- Pitch Deck: {text}
- External Research: {research}
- Financial Summary: {financial_summary}

Follow this structure:

---

**Valuation Summary:**
- Briefly explain the purpose of startup valuation in this context.
- State any assumptions (e.g. years to profitability, TAM, revenue growth rate, margin expectations).

---

**Valuation Methods:**
For each of the following five valuation methods, provide an estimate and a short explanation using reasonable assumptions or inputs from the financial data if available:

1. **Comparable Company Analysis (Comps):**
   - Use relevant startup or public market comparables (industry, region, stage).
   - Suggest a revenue multiple (e.g. 3x–10x) and justify it.

2. **Precedent Transactions:**
   - Refer to recent exits or funding rounds of similar companies.
   - Estimate a valuation based on deal size and stage comparability.

3. **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF):**
   - Use high-level projections to simulate future free cash flows (5 years).
   - Apply a discount rate and terminal value assumption.

4. **Scorecard Method:**
   - Score the company on: team, product, traction, market, competition, and funding environment.
   - Adjust an average seed/pre-seed/post-seed valuation benchmark accordingly.

5. **Venture Capital Method (VCM):**
   - Estimate exit value in 5–7 years (e.g., using projected ARR and exit multiples).
   - Work backwards using expected IRR (e.g., 25–35%) to get today’s valuation.

---

**ARR Growth Simulation:**
- Based on uploaded financials or assumptions, estimate what operational improvements are needed to reach:
  - $50M ARR in 5 years
  - $100M ARR in 5 years
- Suggest headcount, market expansion, or product developments that might be required.

---

**Valuation Range and Recommendation:**
- Combine all methods to produce a final suggested valuation range.
- State whether this is aligned with the startup’s ask or expected round.
- Make a recommendation: invest, pass, or negotiate terms.

Be analytical and realistic — if key data is missing, make thoughtful estimates and clearly state assumptions.
